## 1. Greetings & Introductions

### Common Greetings
| French | English | Usage |
|--------|---------|-------|
| **Bonjour** | Hello / Good day | Formal/informal, daytime |
| **Bonsoir** | Good evening | Evening, after sunset |
| **Bonne nuit** | Good night | Before sleeping |
| **Salut** | Hi / Bye | Informal, friends/family |
| **Au revoir** | Goodbye | Standard farewell |
| **À bientôt** | See you soon | Informal |
| **À demain** | See you tomorrow | Specific date |

### Self-Introduction
```text
Bonjour, je m'appelle [Prénom] [Nom].
(Good day, my name is [First Name] [Last Name].)

Enchanté. (Nice to meet you.)

Je viens de [Ville/Pays]. (I am from [City/Country].)
```

### Key Phrases
- **Merci:** Thank you
- **S'il vous plaît:** Please (formal)
- **S'il te plaît:** Please (informal)
- **De rien:** You're welcome
- **Non:** No
- **Oui:** Yes
- **Pardon:** Excuse me / Pardon me

## 2. Numbers & Counting (0-100)

### Numbers 0-12 (Foundation)
| French | English |
|--------|---------|
| 0 | zéro |
| 1 | un / une |
| 2 | deux |
| 3 | trois |
| 4 | quatre |
| 5 | cinq |
| 6 | six |
| 7 | sept |
| 8 | huit |
| 9 | neuf |
| 10 | dix |
| 11 | onze |
| 12 | douze |

### Tens (20, 30, 40, etc.)
| French | English |
|--------|---------|
| 20 | vingt |
| 30 | trente |
| 40 | quarante |
| 50 | cinquante |
| 60 | soixante |
| 70 | soixante-dix (literally: sixty-ten) |
| 80 | quatre-vingts (literally: four-twenties) |
| 90 | quatre-vingt-dix (literally: four-twenties-nine) |
| 100 | cent |

### Forming Numbers 13-16 & 17-19
- 13 = treize, 14 = quatorze, 15 = quinze, 16 = seize
- 17 = dix-sept (ten-seven), 18 = dix-huit (ten-eight), 19 = dix-neuf (ten-nine)

### Forming Numbers 21-99
Structure: `[unit] - et - [tens]` (note: "et" only for 21, 31, 41, 51, 61, 71, 81, 91)
- Example: 21 = `vingt-et-un`
- Example: 35 = `trente-cinq` (no "et" needed)
- Example: 42 = `quarante-deux`
- Example: 67 = `soixante-sept`

### Special Notes on French Numbers
- **70-79:** Use base-20 system: `soixante-dix` (70), `soixante-onze` (71), etc.
- **80-99:** Use base-20 system: `quatre-vingts` (80), `quatre-vingt-un` (81), etc.
- **100+:** `cent`, `cent un` (101), `deux cents` (200), etc.

### Practice: Counting
```
J'ai deux chats. (I have two cats.)
J'attends depuis trente minutes. (I've been waiting for thirty minutes.)
```

## 4. Daily Routine & Time

### Time Expressions
- **le matin:** in the morning
- **l'après-midi:** in the afternoon
- **le soir:** in the evening
- **la nuit:** at night

### Telling Time
- **Quelle heure est-il?** = What time is it?
- **Il est une heure.** = It is one o'clock.
- **Il sont trois heures.** = It is three o'clock.
- **Quart après cinq** = Quarter past five (5:15)
- **Moins le quart** = Quarter to six (5:45 — "less the quarter")
- **Et demie** = Half past (6:30 — "and half")

### Daily Routine Verbs (ER verbs - regular)
| Infinitive | je form | tu form | il/elle form |
|------------|---------|---------|-------------|
| **manger** (to eat) | je **mange** | tu **manges** | il **mange** |
| **boire** (to drink) | je **bois** | tu **bois** | il **boit** |
| **dormir** (to sleep) | je **dors** | tu **dors** | il **dort** |
| **travailler** (to work) | je **travaille** | tu **travailles** | il **travaille** |
| **apprendre** (to learn) | j' **apprends** (stem change) | tu **apprends** | il **apprend** |

### Irregular Verbs: Avoir (to have) & Être (to be)
| Person | avoir | être |
|--------|-------|------|
| je | j' **ai** | je **suis** |
| tu | tu **as** | tu **es** |
| il/elle/on | il **a** | il **est** |
| nous | nous **avons** | nous **sommes** |
| vous | vous **avez** | vous **êtes** |
| ils/elles | ils **ont** | ils **sont** |

### Example Sentences (Present Tense)
```text
J'ai un frère. (I have a brother.)
Elle est étudiante. (She is a student.)
Nous travaillons. (We work.)
```

### Question Formation
```text
Aimes-tu les pommes? (Do you like apples?)
Hab-tu un frère? (Do you have a brother?)
```

## 5. Practical Conversational Situations

### Situation 1: Meeting Someone New
```text
A: Bonjour, je m'appelle Vijay. (Hi, I'm Vijay.)
B: Enchanté. (Nice to meet you.)
A: Comment vous appelez-vous? (What is your name? - formal)
B: Je m'appelle Anika. (My name is Anika.)
A: D'où venez-vous? (Where are you from? - formal)
B: Nous venons d'Allemagne. (We are from Germany.)
A: À bientôt! (See you soon!)
B: Au revoir! (Goodbye!)
```

### Situation 2: Shopping
```text
Client: Bonjour, je cherche un t-shirt. (Hello, I'm looking for a t-shirt.)
Vendeur: Quelle taille portez-vous? (What size do you wear?)
Client: Je porte du M, s'il vous plaît. (Size M, please.)
Vendeur: Ce t-shirt coûte 15 euros. (This t-shirt costs 15 euros.)
Client: Merci au revoir! (Thank you goodbye!)
```

### Situation 3: At a Café/Restaurant
```text
Serveur: Bonsoir, table pour combien? (Good evening, table for how many?)
Client: Table pour deux, s'il vous plaît. (Table for two, please.)
Serveur: Que souhaitez-vous commander? (What would you like to order?)
Client: Je voudrais un café, s'il te plaît. (I'd like a coffee, please.)
Serveur: Et quelque chose à manger? (And something to eat?)
Client: Je prendrais la salade. (I'll have the salad.)
Serveur: Très bien, je reviens dans quelques minutes. (Very well, I'll be back in a few minutes.)
```

## 6. Key Grammar Rules

### Gender (Le, La, Les)
All French nouns have gender (masculine, feminine). Learn with the article:
- **le** professeur (the teacher) — masculine
- **la** professeure (the teacher) — feminine
- **les** professeurs (the teachers) — plural (either gender)

### Plural Forms
Most nouns add **-s** or **-x**:
- **le chat** → **les chats** (cat → cats)
- **le garçon** → **les garçons** (boy → boys)
- **la fille** → **les filles** (girl → girls)
- **le prix** → **les prix** (price → prices)

Some exceptions: -ail → -aules (le journal → les journaux), -al → -aux (le salon → les salons).

### Negation (ne ... pas)
```text
Je **ne** **mange** **pas** de viande. (I don't eat meat.)
Il **ne** **boit** **pas** d'alcool. (He doesn't drink alcohol.)
```
- In spoken French, "ne" is often dropped: `J'ai pas` instead of `Je ne ai pas`

### Partitive Articles (de/d')
Used for uncountable foods/drinks:
- **du** (de + le) = some (masculine)
- **de la** (de + la) = some (feminine)
- **des** (de + les) = some (plural)
- **d'** (before vowel/ha) = some (any gender)

```text
Je veux **du** pain. (I want some bread - masculine)
Je veux **de la** eau. (I want some water - feminine)
Je veux **des** pommes. (I want some apples - plural)
```

### Partitive Negation
```text
Je **n'ai** **pas** **de** pain. (I don't have any bread.)
Il ne boit **d'**eau. (He doesn't drink any water.)
```

## 7. Practical Tips for A1 Learners

### Daily Practice (15-20 min)
1. **Review vocabulary** (use Anki or flashcards with spaced repetition)
2. **Grammar exercise** (one topic per day - focus on present tense first)
3. **Read aloud** (10 minutes: practice pronunciation and intonation)
4. **Write 3-5 sentences** (apply what you learned that day)
5. **Listen to French** (podcasts, songs, slow news)

### Recommended Resources
- **Duolingo:** Gamified daily lessons (free)
- **Français Facile:** Grammar lessons and exercises (free)
- **TV5 Monde:** French learning with videos (free)
- **Anki:** Spaced repetition flashcards (free, open-source)
- **French Podcasts:** "Coffee Break French", "News in Slow French"

### Common Pitfalls & Fixes
| Pitfall | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Mixing formal/informal | Learn both forms early; use "vous" for unknown adults, "tu" for friends/children |
| Noun gender confusion | Always learn with article (le/la); use gendered flashcards |
| Numbers 70-99 (base-20 system) | Practice regularly; memorize the pattern |
| Partitive articles (de) | Learn with food/drink vocabulary; practice negation forms |
| Verb conjugation memorization | Start with -ER verbs (regular), then move to irregular |

### Pronunciation Guide
- **French "r":** Uvular fricative (back of throat, not rolled like Spanish)
- **French "u":** Close front rounded lip position (like "ee" but lips rounded)
- **Nasal vowels:** "an", "en", "in", "on", "un" — air flows through nose + mouth
- **Silent letters:** Many French words end with silent consonants (d, g, t, x, s)
