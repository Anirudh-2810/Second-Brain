# Handbook on Productivity (Asian Productivity Organization)

> **Source:** [[raw-sources/_extracted/Handbook-on-Productivity-2015.txt]] (A. D. Kalaw Jr. et al., *Handbook on Productivity*, APO, 2015).

The organizational/formal backbone of the theme: a rigorous definition, a management cycle, and a taxonomy of 31 productivity-enhancing initiatives grouped by the **Four P's** (People, Product, Process, Policy). Personal-productivity sources ([[:overview]]) are the individual-scale implementation of these.

---

## 1. The Definition

$$Productivity = \frac{Output}{Input} \qquad \text{and} \qquad Productivity = Efficiency + Effectiveness$$

- **Efficiency** — doing things right (minimize input for given output).
- **Effectiveness** — doing the right things (attaining desired outcomes; customer satisfaction ⇒ repeat revenue).
- Objective: **maximize output and minimize input**. The concept has widened from cost/quality to social, green, and knowledge productivity.
- **Improvement is a managed, systematic process** — it does not happen by coincidence.

---

## 2. The Productivity Management Cycle (PDCA)

The cycle runs **Check → Action → Plan → Do** and repeats forever (this is the [[productive-flowchart]] loop in organizational form):

1. **Check** — assess the present condition / productivity level.
2. **Action** — find countermeasures / improvements.
3. **Plan** — pick the program/project to implement.
4. **Do** — implement; then **gainshare** and **Check** again.

> This is *Kaizen*: continuous, never-ending improvement with everyone participating, eliminating **muri** (overload), **muda** (waste), and **mura** (inconsistency).

---

## 3. The Four P's (impact areas)

| P | Focus | Representative initiatives |
|---|---|---|
| **People** | Raise worker efficiency/effectiveness | Employee suggestion schemes, Lean (Toyota) management, OHSAS 18000, SA 8000, Workplace cooperation, Quality circles |
| **Product** | Improve product quality/responsiveness | 3Rs, Customer Satisfaction Index, Eco-design, HACCP, Niche marketing, QMS, Supply Chain Mgmt |
| **Process** | Make planning→design→production→delivery efficient | Business Process Reengineering, Just-in-Time, Preventive/Productive Maintenance, Six Sigma |
| **Policy** | Improve the overall environment | Balanced Scorecard, Business Excellence frameworks, CSR, Energy/Environmental Mgmt, ISO 9000, National Quality Award |

**Cross-cutting** (span several P's): **5S/Good Housekeeping**, **7 Wastes**, **Benchmarking**, **Green Productivity**, **Kaizen**, **Knowledge Management**, **Quality Circles**.

---

## 4. Selected Initiative Cheat-Sheet

- **5S / Good Housekeeping** — Sort, Set in order, Shine, Standardize, Sustain. *Untidy work areas are not productive*; foundation of all quality programs.
- **7 Wastes (muda)** — waiting, transporting, processing, inventory, motion, defects/rework, overproduction (Taiichi Ohno, Toyota).
- **Kaizen** — never-ending improvement by everyone; core of Japanese management.
- **Lean / Toyota Production System** — do more with less (time, inventory, space, labor, money).
- **Six Sigma** — near-elimination of defects via statistical tools; breakthrough quantum quality gains.
- **Just-in-Time** — produce only what's needed, just as much as needed, when needed.
- **Business Process Reengineering** — radical redesign from scratch for dramatic cost/quality/service/speed gains.
- **Balanced Scorecard** — balance financial + customer + process + learning perspectives (Kaplan & Norton).
- **Knowledge Management** — create, share, apply knowledge as the intangible production factor.

## 5. Individual-Scale Translation

- **5S → workspace & digital decluttering** ([[focus-minimalism-babauta]]; [[101-ways-workplace-productivity-fishel]] tidying).
- **7 Wastes → your day** — identify *waiting* (load times, commuting), *motion* (context switching, [[deep-work-attention-economics]] attention residue), *defects/rework* ("get it right first time"), *overproduction* (busy-ness that isn't accomplishment, [[little-book-productivity-scott-young]] "reward laziness").
- **PDCA → weekly review** — Check ([[gtd-task-management]] Reflect), Plan (next MITs), Do (execute), Act (improve) each week.
- **Gainsharing → reward yourself** — "pat yourself on the back" (Fishel); celebrate completions ([[atomic-habits-systems]] "make it satisfying").
