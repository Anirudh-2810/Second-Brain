# Critical Media Consumption — Reading a Persuasive Video Essay

Documentary-essay YouTube is not neutral. Length, voice-of-God narration, and cited clips feel like evidence; an essay is still a **persuasive argument**. Consuming responsibly = separating (1) documented fact, (2) allegation/reported claim, (3) interpretation, then deciding what each bucket is good for.

## The 3-Bucket Claim Audit (reusable)

Before distilling any persuasive essay into this vault:

1. **Bucket 1 — Documented fact:** independently verifiable (court record, dated primary source, broadcast artifact, self-published doctrine). Verifiable here and now.
2. **Bucket 2 — Allegation / reported claim:** someone credible *says* X happened, but it is unadjudicated, denied, or testimony-only. Verifiable that "person P claims X" — not that X happened.
3. **Bucket 3 — Interpretation:** the author's argument, analogy, framing, or stated belief about what the facts *mean*. This is where the essay's actual thesis lives.

Then apply three filters:

- **Provenance & impossibility first** (from [[synthetic-media-literacy]]'s 7-point checklist): is the subject alive? Is there a real byline/channel with an editorial record? Book fear: "live subject + legit channel + dated events" → ingestible as a *document of the essay*, never as an authoritative biography.
- **Confidence-mark every bucket-2 and bucket-3 claim** (`(stated)` / `(alleged)` / `(inference, x)` / `(TBC)`).
- **Never state the essay's conclusion as a fact of the vault.** The 3-bucket table below is the repeatable pattern.

## Case study: "The Real American Psycho: Tom Cruise" (Patrick Cc:)

- **Source confidence:** legitimate commentary channel (2.54M subs, real author, linked secondary sources such as Janet Reitman's *Inside Scientology*, Mike Rinder's *A Billion Years*, Vox reporting). NOT a fabrication (contrast: [[synthetic-media-literacy]]'s Epstein video).
- **The essay's thesis (bucket 3, flagged):** framing Cruise via Christian Bale's documented "nothing behind the eyes" Bateman remark, the essay argues Cruise is either hostage-to, exploiter-of, or brainwashed-by Scientology — the essay itself ends by asking the viewer to pick. This is argument, not finding.
- **Ingestion rule applied:** the video's *documented* content (Scientology structure, Cruise's public record) may enter the vault with `(stated)`; testimony-based material enters as allegations with the accuser named; the psycho-framing enters only as the essayist's interpretation.

### Bucket 3 — Interpretation (essayist's argument, NOT vault fact)

- The **"American Psycho" framing itself** — Bale's remark is about public performance, not a clinical diagnosis; equating it to Cruise's actual psychology is the author's hook.
- "Cruise is in too deep (hostage), a knowing exploiter, or a brainwashed victim" — the essay's closing trilemma, explicitly left to the viewer.
- Value judgments: "whole lot of nothing," "trained to lie to yourself," the church as a "self-fulfilling-prophesy paywall."

## Why this matters for the vault

- **Satirizing a belief ≠ documenting a belief:** South Park's Xenu breakdown used by the essay is accurate *about the doctrine* — quoting a parody as evidence is fine when the doctrine itself is the target, but the two must not fuse.
- **Live subject + defamation-shaped content:** Cruise is alive; unlike the Epstein case there is no impossibility refutation, so the discipline shifts to *naming the accuser* (Rinder, Reitman, ex-members) and labeling the thesis — never presenting either as this vault's finding.
- **The pattern scales:** education content, political essays, ad-funded "documentaries" all pass through the same 3 buckets before becoming `wiki/` knowledge.
