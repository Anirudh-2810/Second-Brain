## 1. SAT — Scholastic Aptitude Test Overview

### SAT Structure (as of 2024)
| Section | Time | Questions | Score Range |
|---------|------|-----------|-------------|
| **Reading & Writing** | 64 min | 54 questions | 200-800 |
| **Math** | 70 min | 44 questions | 200-800 |
| **Total** | 2 hours 14 min | 98 questions | 400-1600 |

### Reading & Writing Sections
| Content Domain | What's Tested |
|----------------|---------------|
| **Craft & Structure** | Word meanings, text analysis, rhetorical synthesis |
| **Information & Ideas** | Central ideas, relationships, quantitative analysis |
| **Standard English Conventions** | Grammar, punctuation, sentence structure, usage |
| **Cross-Text Connections** | Comparing arguments, synthesizing information |

### Math Sections
| Content Domain | Topics |
|----------------|--------|
| **Algebra** | Linear equations/inequalities, systems, function notation |
| **Problem-Solving & Data Analysis** | Ratios, percentages, proportional reasoning, statistics |
| **Advanced Math** | Quadratic equations, nonlinear functions, exponential growth |
| **Geometry & Trigonometry** | Area, volume, circles, triangles, right-triangle trig |

### SAT Scoring
- **Raw Score:** Number of correct answers (no penalty for guessing)
- **Scaled Score:** Converted to 200-800 per section
- **Total Score:** Sum of both sections (400-1600)
- **Percentile Rank:** How you scored compared to other test-takers

### SAT Preparation Timeline (12-Week Plan)
```
Weeks 1-2: Diagnostic test, identify weak areas, set target score
Weeks 3-4: Focused study on Reading & Writing fundamentals
Weeks 5-6: Focused study on Math fundamentals
Weeks 7-8: Mixed practice tests (alternating sections)
Weeks 9-10: Full-length practice tests (timed, realistic conditions)
Weeks 11-12: Review mistakes, fine-tune test strategy, rest/recover
```

### SAT Registration
- **College Board website:** collegeboard.org/sat
- **Test Dates:** 7 times per year (typically March, May, June, August, October, November, December)
- **Fee:** ~$60 (plus regional fees), fee waivers available for eligible students
- **Required Items:** Photo ID, admission ticket, calculator (for Math section)

## 2. SAT — Reading & Writing Preparation

### Reading: Craft & Structure
- **Word Meanings:** Context-dependent vocabulary; use surrounding sentences to infer meaning
- **Text Analysis:** Identify author's purpose, tone, main claim, evidence
- **Rhetorical Synthesis:** Combine information from multiple sources (single question)

### Reading: Information & Ideas
- **Central Ideas:** Identify the primary purpose/argument of a passage
- **Relationships:** Understand how different parts of the text connect
- **Quantitative Analysis:** Interpret graphs, tables, charts embedded in text

### Reading: Standard English Conventions
- **Grammar:** Subject-verb agreement, pronoun consistency, parallel structure
- **Punctuation:** Commas, semicolons, colons, dashes usage
- **Sentence Structure:** Avoiding fragments, run-on sentences, proper coordination/subordination
- **Usage:** Correct word choice, idioms, commonly confused words (affect/effect, their/there/they're)

### Writing: Text-Based Arguments
- **Make Claims:** Write defensible claims about the text
- **Support Claims:** Use evidence directly from the passage
- **Reasoning:** Explain how evidence supports the claim
- **Synthesize:** Combine multiple sources' arguments (if multiple passages)

### Sample Reading Question Strategy
1. **Read the question first** — know what to look for
2. **Locate the relevant passage section** — skim for keywords
3. **Eliminate wrong answers** — three out of four will have fatal flaws
4. **Choose the best remaining answer** — must be fully supported by text

### Sample Writing Question Strategy
1. **Identify the error type** (grammar, punctuation, usage, organization)
2. **Read surrounding sentences** — context matters
3. **Apply the rule** — does the underlined portion follow standard English?
4. **Choose the clearest correction** — when multiple options are grammatically correct, pick the most concise/effective

## 3. SAT — Math Preparation

### Algebra Focus
| Topic | Key Formulas |
|-------|--------------|
| **Linear Equations** | `y = mx + b` (slope-intercept), `Point-Slope: y - y1 = m(x - x1)` |
| **Systems of Equations** | Substitution method, elimination method, graphing method |
| **Linear Inequalities** | Graphing boundary lines, shading solution regions |
| **Function Notation** | `f(x) = 2x + 3`, evaluate `f(5)`, find `x` when `f(x) = 11` |

### Practice Problem
```
Solve for x: 2x + 5 = 15
→ 2x = 10
→ x = 5
```

### Systems of Equations Practice
```
Solve: 
  x + y = 10
  x - y = 2
→ Add: 2x = 12 → x = 6
→ Substitute: 6 + y = 10 → y = 4
→ Solution: (x, y) = (6, 4)
```

### Problem-Solving & Data Analysis
| Concept | Key Idea |
|---------|----------|
| **Ratios** | Comparison of two quantities (a:b or a/b) |
| **Proportional Reasoning** | If a/b = c/d, then ad = bc (cross-multiplication) |
| **Percentages** | `part = whole × %`, percentage increase/decrease |
| **Statistics** | Mean (average), median (middle value), mode (most frequent), range (max-min) |

### Practice: Percentages
```
What is 15% of 200?
→ 0.15 × 200 = 30
```

### Percentage Increase
```
Old Value: 80 → New Value: 100
Increase: 100 - 80 = 20
% Increase: (20/80) × 100 = 25%
```

### Advanced Math (Quadratic & Exponential)
| Topic | Key Formula |
|-------|-------------|
| **Quadratic Equations** | `ax² + bx + c = 0`, quadratic formula: `x = (-b ± √(b² - 4ac)) / 2a` |
| **Factoring** | `x² + 5x + 6 = (x + 2)(x + 3)` → roots at x = -2, -3 |
| **Exponential Growth** | `y = a(1 + r)ⁿ` where a = initial, r = rate, n = time periods |
| **Exponential Decay** | `y = a(1 - r)ⁿ` |

### Practice: Quadratic Formula
```
Solve: x² - 5x + 6 = 0
→ a=1, b=-5, c=6
→ x = (5 ± √(25 - 24)) / 2
→ x = (5 ± 1) / 2
→ x = 3 or x = 2
```

### Geometry & Trigonometry
| Topic | Key Formula |
|-------|-------------|
| **Area of Triangle** | `A = 1/2 × base × height` |
| **Pythagorean Theorem** | `a² + b² = c²` (right triangles) |
| **Circle Area** | `A = πr²` |
| **Circle Circumference** | `C = 2πr` or `πd` |
| **Special Triangles** | 45-45-90: sides ratio 1:1:√2; 30-60-90: sides ratio 1:√3:2 |
| **Right Triangle Trig** | `sin(θ) = opposite/hypotenuse`, `cos(θ) = adjacent/hypotenuse`, `tan(θ) = opposite/adjacent` |

### Practice: Pythagorean Theorem
```
Find the hypotenuse of a right triangle with legs 3 and 4.
→ a² + b² = c²
→ 3² + 4² = c²
→ 9 + 16 = c²
→ 25 = c²
→ c = 5
```

## 4. Ankur Warikoo Study Abroad Series (3 Parts)

### Context
Ankur Warikoo is a popular Indian educator and entrepreneur who created a 3-part series on studying abroad. Based on the PDFs found in the user's Desktop Study abroad folder.

### Part 1: Planning Your Study Abroad
**Key Topics Covered:**
- Self-assessment: Why do you want to study abroad?
- Country/University selection criteria
- Financial planning (tuition + living expenses)
- Timeline creation (when to start applying)
- Standardized test requirements (SAT, IELTS/TOEFL, GRE/GMAT)
- Application components (essay, LORs, transcripts)

**Key Takeaways from Warikoo:**
1. Start planning **18-24 months** before intended start date
2. **Rank 5-7 universities** based on: fit, budget, placement, location
3. **Budget realistically**: include tuition, rent, food, insurance, travel, emergencies
4. **Test preparation** should begin **12-18 months** in advance
5. **Scholarship applications** have earlier deadlines than admission

### Part 2: The Application Process
**Key Topics Covered:**
- Writing the personal statement/essay
- Requesting Letters of Recommendation (LORs)
- Completing the online application forms
- Transcript evaluation and translation
- Application fee waivers
- Interview preparation (if required)

**Key Takeaways from Warikoo:**
1. **Essay is 30% of admission decision** — invest significant time
2. **LORs should be specific:** recommenders should give concrete examples
3. **Be authentic** in essays — don't write what you think they want to hear
4. **Track every deadline** in a spreadsheet with reminders 2 weeks before
5. **Apply to at least 3 "safety" schools** where admission likelihood is high

### Part 3: Adjusting Abroad & Beyond
**Key Topics Covered:**
- Visa application process and requirements
- Pre-departure preparation (packing, housing, health insurance)
- Arrival: airport pickup, accommodation check-in
- Cultural adjustment (homesickness, culture shock)
- Managing finances abroad (bank accounts, currency, budgeting)
- Part-time work options (student visa restrictions)
- Building a support network (international student associations)
- Long-term career planning (internships, post-study work options)

**Key Takeaways from Warikoo:**
1. **Housing is priority #1** — secure before arrival if possible
2. **Open a local bank account** within first 2 weeks
3. **Join international student groups** immediately (Facebook, university clubs)
4. **Keep emergency contacts:** embassy, university international office, local friends
5. **Maintain connections** with home (regular video calls, but don't isolate)
6. **Track expenses** in a spreadsheet (budgeting is crucial without parental support)

### Universal Checklist (from all 3 parts)
| Timeline | Task |
|----------|------|
| **18-24 months before** | Research universities, take standardized tests, start savings plan |
| **12-18 months before** | Finalize university list, take tests again if needed, request LORs |
| **6-12 months before** | Submit applications, apply for scholarships, begin visa process |
| **3-6 months before** | Accept admission offer, apply for student visa, book flights |
| **1-3 months before** | Book accommodation, arrange health insurance, pack strategically |
| **1 week before** | Confirm housing, share arrival details with family, finalize budget |
| **Day 1** | Arrive, settle in, register with international office, open bank account |

## 5. Visa Requirements (General Overview)

### Student Visa Types
| Country | Visa Type | Key Requirements |
|---------|-----------|------------------|
| **USA** | F-1 Visa | I-20 form, proof of funds, SEVIS fee, visa interview |
| **UK** | Student Visa | CAS letter, financial evidence, English language test |
| **Canada** | Study Permit | Letter of acceptance, proof of funds, biometrics |
| **Australia** | Student Subclass 500 | CoE (Confirmable eOffer), GTE requirement, OSHC insurance |
| **Germany** | Student Visa | University admission, blocked account (Sperrkonto) €11,208/year |

### Financial Proof Requirements
- **USA (F-1):** Must show access to **1st year + 1 month** of expenses
- **UK Student Visa:** Must show **£30,396+** for London (outside London lower)
- **Canada Study Permit:** Must show **CAD $20,635+** (plus tuition + first year)
- **Australia Student Visa:** Must show **AUD $29,700+** (single, outside Sydney lower)

### English Language Tests
| Test | Score Required (Typical) | Duration | Cost |
|------|-------------------------|----------|------|
| **IELTS** | 6.0-7.0 overall | 2 hours 45 min | ~$250-280 |
| **TOEFL iBT** | 60-90 total score | 3 hours | ~$245-260 |
| **Duolingo English Test** | 95-120 total | 1 hour | ~$50-70 |

### Test Preparation Timeline
- **Minimum:** 3-4 months of dedicated study
- **Ideal:** 6-8 months for first-time test-takers
- **Retake:** Wait at least 12 days between IELTS/TOEFL attempts
- **Score Validity:** 2 years from test date

## 6. Pre-Departure Checklist

### Documentation
- [ ] Valid passport (expires > 6 months after planned return)
- [ ] Student visa (printed + digital copies)
- [ ] University admission letter (printed + digital)
- [ ] Financial documents (bank statements, scholarship letters)
- [ ] I-20/CAS/Letter of Acceptance (depending on country)
- [ ] Vaccination records (if required)
- [ ] Travel insurance documentation

### Financial Preparation
- [ ] Open international bank account (or home country account with global access)
- [ ] Notify bank of travel plans (prevents card freezing)
- [ ] Bring some local currency (cash + for immediate expenses)
- [ ] Set up online banking access
- [ ] Understand currency exchange rates
- [ ] Bring 2 debit/credit cards (different banks, in case one gets frozen)

### Housing
- [ ] Confirm accommodation (on-campus dorm or off-campus apartment)
- [ ] Share contact with roommates (if applicable)
- [ ] Understand lease terms and utility responsibilities
- [ ] Research neighborhood safety and transport links

### Health & Wellness
- [ ] Get required vaccinations (check university/visa requirements)
- [ ] Purchase health insurance (many universities mandate student plans)
- [ ] Bring 30-day supply of any prescription medications
- [ ] Research nearby hospitals/urgent care clinics
- [ ] Mental health preparation (culture shock is real)

### Packing Smart
- **Essentials (carry-on):** Documents, medications, one change of clothes, phone/laptop + chargers
- **Season-appropriate clothing** for destination climate
- **Electronics:** Laptop, phone, portable charger, power adapter (country-specific)
- **Personal items:** Photos of home, favorite snacks, comfort items
- **NOT recommended:** Heavy books (buy locally), hair dryers/styling tools (often dual-voltage check), excessive clothing

### Cultural Preparation
- [ ] Learn basic phrases in local language (greetings, "thank you," "where is")
- [ ] Research cultural norms (tipping, dress code, social etiquette)
- [ ] Understand local transportation (metro, bus, train systems)
- [ ] Download useful apps (maps, translation, ride-sharing, local events)
- [ ] Set expectations with family back home (communication frequency)

## 7. Post-Arrival Survival Guide

### First Week Priorities
1. **Register with international student office** — get your student ID, campus map
2. **Open a local bank account** — bring passport, visa, university letter, proof of address
3. **Secure housing** — confirm check-in, meet roommates, understand bills
4. **Get a local SIM card** — easier than relying on roaming
5. **Attend orientation events** — meet other international students

### Cultural Adjustment Phases
```
Phase 1: Honeymoon (0-2 weeks) — Everything is new and exciting
Phase 2: Culture Shock (2-6 weeks) — Frustration, homesickness, different norms
Phase 3: Adjustment (6-12 weeks) — Starting to adapt, finding routines
Phase 4: Mastery (3-6 months+) — Feeling comfortable, functioning well
```

### Common Challenges & Solutions
| Challenge | Solution |
|-----------|----------|
| **Homesickness** | Regular but scheduled calls with family; bring familiar items (photos, snacks) |
| **Language Barrier** | Use translation apps initially; practice local language daily (15 min) |
| **Financial Stress** | Track every expense in spreadsheet; use student discounts |
| **Acculturation Stress** | Join international student clubs; attend campus events |
| **Academic Pressure** | Visit professor office hours early; form study groups |
| **Homesickness for Food** | Cook familiar meals occasionally; explore local grocery stores |

### Building a Support Network
- **International Student Office:** Primary resource for visa, housing, academic issues
- **Student Organizations:** Cultural clubs, hobby groups, sports teams
- **Classmates/Roommates:** Daily interactions, study partners
- **Professors/Academic Advisors:** Academic guidance, career advice
- **Local Friends:** Community members, neighbors, language exchange partners

### Post-Graduation Options
| Option | Description | Typical Timeline |
|--------|-------------|------------------|
| **Optional Practical Training (OPT)** | Work in field of study for 12 months (USA) | Apply within 90 days of graduation |
| **STEM OPT Extension** | Additional 24 months for STEM degrees (USA) | Must apply before OPT expires |
| **Post-Study Work Visa** | Work 2-3 years after graduation (UK, Canada, Australia) | Varies by country |
| **Further Studies (Master's/PhD)** | Continue academic path | Apply 6-12 months before graduation |
| **Return Home** | Apply domestic job market or entrepreneurship | Plan 3-6 months before graduation |
