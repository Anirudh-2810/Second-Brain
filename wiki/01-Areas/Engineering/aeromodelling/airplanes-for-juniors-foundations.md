## 1. Dynamic pressure intuition (P1)

Speed doubled → dynamic pressure quadrupled. That single scaling explains why lift, drag, and control authority all explode with airspeed, and why take-off/landing speeds are the whole game. Quote it when asked why small speed changes matter so much.

## 2. Vortices from edges (P1)

Air hitting a plate at angle spills over the edge from the high-pressure side to the low-pressure side and rolls into a vortex. Zero AoA edge-on flow is clean; angled flow always leaks at edges. Use this to explain wingtip vortices and induced drag in one breath.

## 3. Drag-reduction ladder (P2)

- Separation drag: streamline shapes, delay separation (the 737-to-F-104 comparison set in the book).
- Angle-of-attack drag: raise aspect ratio (span ÷ average chord); long skinny wings pay less for the same lift.
- Wave drag: thin wings first, then sweep and area-ruling as speed climbs (P-80 → F-104 lineage in the book).
- Say as a ladder: clean the shape, stretch the wing, thin the section.

## 4. Turn balance (P3)

Tighter turn → more bank needed so the combined gravity + centripetal push still goes straight down through the seat. Uncoordinated turns skid/slip; coordinated turn keeps the force aligned with the airframe. One-line interview answer for why we bank.

## 5. Pitch stability decalage rule (P3)

Wing and tail incidence difference sets pitch character: too close → twitchy/unstable; right gap → stable; too much → over-stable and sluggish. Name the rule when asked what makes a model hands-off vs twitchy.

## 6. Tube structures (P4)

Lots of height with little material: welded steel tube truss is cheap, light, easy — the book's cheap-stiff answer before composites. Cite when asked how to make a fuselage/boom stiff without weight.
