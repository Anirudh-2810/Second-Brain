## Hardness — definition & units

**C‑01.** Define hardness of water and name the responsible ions.

> [!success]- Answer
> The **soap-destroying / scale-forming** property of water, due to dissolved **Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺ salts**.

**C‑02.** Temporary (carbonate) vs permanent (non-carbonate) hardness — what distinguishes them?

> [!success]- Answer
> Anion **HCO₃⁻** → **temporary** (carbonate, removed by boiling); anion **SO₄²⁻ / Cl⁻** → **permanent** (non-carbonate).

**C‑03.** Universal reference for reporting hardness, and the equivalent weight used?

> [!success]- Answer
> Always report **as CaCO₃** in **mg/L (ppm)**. Eq. weight of CaCO₃ $=\dfrac{100}{2}=50$.

**C‑04.** Which dissolved species do **NOT** cause hardness? (classic trap)

> [!success]- Answer
> **Na⁺/K⁺ salts** (NaCl, NaHCO₃, K₂CO₃…), SiO₂, NaAlO₂, dissolved CO₂, HCl — they cause salinity/alkalinity, not hardness.

**C‑05.** Give the hardness unit conversions (as CaCO₃).

> [!success]- Answer
> $1\ \text{ppm}=1\ \text{mg/L}$ · $1\,°\text{Cl}=14.25\ \text{ppm}$ · $1\,°\text{Fr}=10\ \text{ppm}$ · $1\,°\text{dH}=17.9\ \text{ppm}$ · $1\ \text{ppm}=0.02\ \text{meq/L}$.

**C‑06.** Convert $5\,°\text{Fr}$ and $5\,°\text{dH}$ to ppm.

> [!success]- Answer
> $5\,°\text{Fr}=50\ \text{ppm}$; $5\,°\text{dH}=89.5\ \text{ppm}$.

## Method 1 — EDTA titration

**C‑07.** What is EDTA, and how does it bind Ca²⁺/Mg²⁺?

> [!success]- Answer
> Disodium salt of ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid; a **hexadentate ligand** forming stable **1:1** complexes with Ca²⁺/Mg²⁺.

**C‑08.** EDTA titration — indicator, colour change, and pH buffer?

> [!success]- Answer
> Indicator **EBT (Eriochrome Black-T)**: **wine-red → blue** at endpoint. Buffered to **pH ≈ 10** with **NH₄Cl + NH₄OH**.

**C‑09.** EDTA shortcut: what does $1$ mL EDTA correspond to?

> [!success]- Answer
> $1\ \text{mL EDTA}\equiv\dfrac{50}{V_1}\ \text{mg CaCO}_3$ ($V_1$ = mL sample).

**C‑10.** Total hardness (ppm) formula from the titration?

> [!success]- Answer
> $\text{Total}=\dfrac{V_2}{V_1}\times1000$ ppm ($V_2$ = mL EDTA for the total titration).

**C‑11.** Permanent and temporary hardness from the three titrations?

> [!success]- Answer
> $\text{Permanent}=\dfrac{V_3}{V_1}\times1000$ ppm; $\text{Temporary}=\text{Total}-\text{Permanent}$.

**C‑12.** On a molar basis, what is $1$ mL of $1\ \text{M}$ EDTA equivalent to?

> [!success]- Answer
> **100 mg CaCO₃** (eq. wt 50, and $1\ \text{M}=2\ \text{eq/L}$).

**C‑13.** Why is the sample boiled before the third titration, and what is the reaction?

> [!success]- Answer
> Boiling removes **temporary** hardness: $\mathrm{Ca(HCO_3)_2\xrightarrow{\Delta}CaCO_3\downarrow+H_2O+CO_2}$. The third titration therefore measures **permanent** hardness.

## Method 2 — Zeolite / Permutit

**C‑14.** Formula of sodium zeolite, and what it exchanges?

> [!success]- Answer
> $\mathrm{Na_2O\cdot Al_2O_3\cdot xSiO_2\cdot yH_2O}$ ($x=2$–$10$, $y=2$–$6$); swaps **Na⁺** for Ca²⁺/Mg²⁺.

**C‑15.** Zeolite — the key ratio (NaCl ↔ CaCO₃)?

> [!success]- Answer
> **58.5 mg NaCl ≡ 50 mg CaCO₃**, i.e. **117 g NaCl ↔ 100 g CaCO₃** ($1\ \text{mol NaCl}=1\ \text{eq}=1\ \text{eq CaCO}_3$).

**C‑16.** Residual hardness after zeolite, and one downside?

> [!success]- Answer
> Treated to **~10 ppm** residual; no sludge, **but it adds Na salts** (→ boiler problems).

**C‑17.** Recalled results of the zeolite worked set (Z1–Z4)?

> [!success]- Answer
> Z1 **500 ppm** · Z2 **300 ppm** · Z3 **15,262.5 L** · Z4 **187.2 L**.

## Method 3 — Ion-exchange / Demineralization

**C‑18.** What do the cation and anion resins exchange, and the net reaction?

> [!success]- Answer
> Cation resin **RH⁺** swaps **H⁺** for Ca²⁺/Mg²⁺; anion resin **R'OH** swaps **OH⁻** for Cl⁻/SO₄²⁻/HCO₃⁻; **H⁺ + OH⁻ → H₂O**.

**C‑19.** How are the two resins regenerated?

> [!success]- Answer
> Cation with **HCl**; anion with **NaOH**.

**C‑20.** Residual hardness and the requirement for ion-exchange?

> [!success]- Answer
> Residual **~2 ppm** — best for **high-pressure boilers**; needs **turbidity < 10 ppm**.

**C‑21.** Governing formula for hardness by ion-exchange?

> [!success]- Answer
> $\text{Hardness (ppm as }CaCO_3)=\dfrac{N\times V_{acid}\times 50{,}000}{V_{water}}$ ($N$ = HCl normality, volumes in L).

**C‑22.** Recalled results of the ion-exchange worked set (IE1–IE8)?

> [!success]- Answer
> IE1 **100** · IE2 **37.5** · IE3 **50** · IE4 **40** · IE5 **25** · IE6 **50** ppm · IE7 **160 L** · IE8 **25,000 L**.

## Method 4 — Lime-soda

**C‑23.** Residual hardness for cold vs hot lime-soda process?

> [!success]- Answer
> Cold (room temp, needs coagulant e.g. alum): **50–60 ppm**; Hot (80–150 °C, no coagulant): **15–30 ppm**.

## Industrial effects & boiler troubles

**C‑24.** Causes of boiler corrosion, and how it is prevented?

> [!success]- Answer
> Dissolved **O₂**, **CO₂ → H₂CO₃**, and **MgCl₂ hydrolysis → HCl**. Prevent by **deaeration + Na₂SO₃ scavenger**.

**C‑25.** Caustic embrittlement — cause and prevention?

> [!success]- Answer
> **Na₂CO₃ → NaOH** at boiler temperature, concentrating in cracks → **inter-granular cracking**. Prevent with **phosphate conditioning**, capping NaOH, and tannin/lignin/Na₂SO₄ inhibitors.

**C‑26.** Scale vs sludge — define each.

> [!success]- Answer
> **Scale** = hard adherent deposit on hot walls (CaCO₃, CaSO₄ — inverse solubility, SiO₂); **sludge** = soft loose deposit settling in cool zones (Mg salts, CaCl₂). Both waste fuel and risk explosion.

**C‑27.** One-line effect of hard water on: textile, sugar, paper, laundry?

> [!success]- Answer
> **Textile:** soap waste, bad dye shade · **Sugar:** hindered crystallisation · **Paper:** kills gloss, eats alum · **Laundry:** grey fabric, fibre weakening.

## Exam traps checklist

**C‑28.** Unless told otherwise, how do you report hardness?

> [!success]- Answer
> **As CaCO₃** — never raw Ca/Mg mg/L.

**C‑29.** Which species are excluded from a hardness calculation?

> [!success]- Answer
> **Na/K salts, SiO₂, CO₂** (and HCl, NaAlO₂).

**C‑30.** Relationship between total, permanent, and temporary hardness?

> [!success]- Answer
> $\text{EDTA total}-\text{permanent}=\text{temporary}$; boiling removes only the temporary part.

**C‑31.** What is the zeolite conversion factor (and what is it NOT)?

> [!success]- Answer
> **58.5 → 50** (NaCl → CaCO₃). **Not** 40 or 100.

**C‑32.** What does the ion-exchange formula multiply together?

> [!success]- Answer
> **normality × volume** = equivalents, then **× 50,000 mg/eq**.
