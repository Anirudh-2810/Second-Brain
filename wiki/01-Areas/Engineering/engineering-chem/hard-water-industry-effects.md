# Hard Water — Industry Effects, Rust & Boiler Deposits

## 1. Per-industry effects (exam list)

**Textile.** Soap wasted (no lather); Ca/Mg-soap precipitates stick to fabric → dyed shades go wrong; Fe/Mn/colour/turbidity stain; organics stink.

**Sugar.** Sulphates, nitrates, alkali carbonates hinder crystallisation; sugar turns deliquescent; colour + taste change.

**Dyeing.** Ca/Mg/Fe salts precipitate costly dyes → impure shades, spots; hard water lowers dye solubility; basic dyes precipitate ($\mathrm{Ca^{2+} + HCO_3^- \to CaCO_3\downarrow}$) — uniform dyeing impossible.

**Paper.** Ca/Mg kill the glossy finish and crack paper; alkalinity eats alum ($\mathrm{Al_2(SO_4)_3\cdot14H_2O + 6HCO_3^- \to 2Al(OH)_3 + 6CO_2 + 14H_2O + 3SO_4^{2-}}$) raising cost; Fe/Mn hurt brightness; Ca/Mg raise ash content.

**Laundry.** Needs soft, colour/Fe/Mn-free water; hard water → more soap, grey-yellow shade, fibre weakening (tears), stiff fabric, extra rinse cycles.

**Pharma.** Needs ultra-pure (colourless, odourless, tasteless, pathogen-free) water; hard water ruins drugs/injections/ointments; WHO: hard supply must be softened → production cost up.

**Beverage.** Must be contaminant/microbe-free and non-alkaline (alkali neutralises fruit acids, kills taste); hard water shifts colour/taste/odour and leaves deposits — treat before use.

## 2. Rust formation in pipes

$\mathrm{Ca(HCO_3)_2 \to CaCO_3 + H_2O + CO_2}$ (heat) — $\mathrm{CaCO_3}}$ deposits in pipes while released $\mathrm{CO_2 + H_2O \to H_2CO_3}$ (carbonic acid) corrodes them. Deposit + corrosion together weaken metal under high T/P.

## 3. Boiler corrosion vs caustic embrittlement

| | Boiler corrosion | Caustic embrittlement |
|---|---|---|
| Nature | General chemical/electrochemical attack | Specific: conc. NaOH damage |
| Causes | Dissolved $\mathrm{O_2}$ ($4\mathrm{Fe}+3\mathrm{O_2}+6\mathrm{H_2O}\to4\mathrm{Fe(OH)_3}$), $\mathrm{CO_2}$ ($\to \mathrm{H_2CO_3}$), acids, MgCl₂ hydrolysis ($\to$ HCl — "particularly dangerous") | $\mathrm{Na_2CO_3 + H_2O \to 2NaOH + CO_2}$ at boiler T; NaOH concentrates in cracks, forms $\mathrm{Na_2FeO_2}$ (reversible cycle keeps eating Fe) |
| Damage | Gradual thinning, pitting, holes | Brittle inter-granular cracking at rivets/bends/stressed zones |
| Prevention | Deaeration, $\mathrm{Na_2SO_3}$ scavenger ($2\mathrm{Na_2SO_3+O_2}\to2\mathrm{Na_2SO_4}$), alkaline pH, good feed water | Phosphate over carbonate conditioning, cap NaOH, tannin/lignin/$\mathrm{Na_2SO_4}$ inhibitors, feed-water treatment |

## 4. Scale vs sludge formation

Evaporation concentrates salts past saturation → precipitate. **Scale** = hard, adherent, on hot walls (CaCO₃ from bicarbonates, CaSO₄ by inverse solubility — worst in HP boilers, SiO₂); **sludge** = soft, loose, settles in cool zones/bends (MgCO₃, MgSO₄, MgCl₂, CaCl₂). Scale removal can need hammer-and-chisel; sludge blows down. Costs of either: fuel waste, lower safety/efficiency, explosion risk.

## 5. Non-hardness-causing substances (do NOT count)

Na⁺/K⁺ salts (NaCl, NaHCO₃, K₂CO₃, KHCO₃, KBr), SiO₂, NaAlO₂, dissolved gases (CO₂), acids (HCl) — they cause salinity/alkalinity/acidity, not hardness. Classic exam trap.
