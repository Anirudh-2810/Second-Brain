# MSE Recall Test — Chemistry Module 1 (Water Technology)

Pack: [[mse-revision-chem-m1-water]] · Cards: [[mse-flashcards-chem-m1]]

> **How to use:** answer cold, then reveal. Always report hardness **as CaCO₃**; state the formula before substituting.

## Q1 — Unit conversions

Convert **250 ppm (as CaCO₃)** into °Clark, °French, °German, and meq/L.

> [!success]- Answer
> $°\text{Cl}=250/14.25=17.5$ · $°\text{Fr}=250/10=25$ · $°\text{dH}=250/17.9=14.0$ · $\text{meq/L}=250\times0.02=5$.

## Q2 — EDTA: total, permanent, temporary

A **50 mL** hard-water sample needed **15 mL** EDTA for total hardness and **5 mL** for permanent (after boiling). Find total, permanent, and temporary hardness in ppm as CaCO₃.

> [!success]- Answer
> Total $=\dfrac{15}{50}\times1000=\mathbf{300\ ppm}$; Permanent $=\dfrac{5}{50}\times1000=\mathbf{100\ ppm}$; Temporary $=300-100=\mathbf{200\ ppm}$.

## Q3 — EDTA principle

State the indicator, the colour change, and the buffering pH used in the EDTA titration.

> [!success]- Answer
> Indicator **EBT (Eriochrome Black-T)**, **wine-red → blue**; buffered to **pH ≈ 10** with **NH₄Cl + NH₄OH**. EDTA is a hexadentate ligand forming 1:1 complexes with Ca²⁺/Mg²⁺.

## Q4 — Zeolite: NaCl requirement

Softening **10,000 L** of water of hardness **500 ppm (as CaCO₃)** by the zeolite process requires how much NaCl?

> [!success]- Answer
> CaCO₃ to remove $=500\times10000=5\times10^{6}\ \text{mg}=5000\ \text{g}$. Ratio **117 g NaCl ↔ 100 g CaCO₃** $\Rightarrow \text{NaCl}=5000\times\frac{117}{100}=\mathbf{5850\ g\ (\approx5.85\ kg)}$.

## Q5 — Ion-exchange: hardness from titration

**500 L** of water is treated through a cation resin; titration of the effluent acid used **25 L of 0.02 N HCl**. Find the hardness.

> [!success]- Answer
> $H=\dfrac{N\,V_{acid}\times50000}{V_{water}}=\dfrac{0.02\times25\times50000}{500}=\mathbf{50\ ppm\ as\ CaCO_3}$.

## Q6 — Lime-soda comparison

Give the residual hardness for the cold and hot lime-soda processes, and say which is used with a coagulant.

> [!success]- Answer
> Cold (room temp, **needs coagulant** e.g. alum): **50–60 ppm**; Hot (80–150 °C, no coagulant): **15–30 ppm**.

## Q7 — Which process for a high-pressure boiler?

Zeolite or ion-exchange — which is preferred for a high-pressure boiler, and why?

> [!success]- Answer
> **Ion-exchange (demineralisation)** — residual **~2 ppm**, and it also removes **anions** (Cl⁻/SO₄²⁻), unlike zeolite which leaves Na salts that cause boiler problems. (Needs turbidity < 10 ppm.)

## Q8 — Caustic embrittlement

Explain **caustic embrittlement** and give two preventive measures.

> [!success]- Answer
> **Na₂CO₃ → NaOH** at boiler temperature; NaOH concentrates in cracks/cold-worked zones → **inter-granular cracking**. Prevent by **phosphate conditioning**, capping NaOH (avoiding free alkali), and tannin/lignin/Na₂SO₄ inhibitors.

## Q9 — Scale vs sludge

Differentiate **scale** and **sludge**, giving one example of each.

> [!success]- Answer
> **Scale** = hard, adherent, on **hot** surfaces (e.g. CaCO₃, CaSO₄ — inverse solubility); **sludge** = soft, loose, settles in **cool/quiet** zones (e.g. Mg(OH)₂, CaCl₂).

## Q10 — Hardness exclusion

A water sample contains Ca(HCO₃)₂, MgSO₄, NaCl, SiO₂, and dissolved CO₂. Which contribute to hardness, and which do **not**?

> [!success]- Answer
> Contribute: **Ca(HCO₃)₂** (temporary) and **MgSO₄** (permanent). Do **not**: **NaCl** (Na salt), **SiO₂**, **dissolved CO₂** — they cause salinity/alkalinity, not hardness.
