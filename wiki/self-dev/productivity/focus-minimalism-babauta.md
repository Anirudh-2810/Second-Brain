# Focus & Minimalism (Leo Babauta)

> **Source:** [[raw-sources/_extracted/focus-leo-babauta-6269.txt]] (Leo Babauta, *Focus: A Simplicity Manifesto in the Age of Distraction*).

The key to productivity, health, and peace in the Age of Distraction is a single skill: **focus**. Not "less is more" — **less is better**. Choose the essential, protect it, and produce.

---

## 1. The Diagnosis: The Age of Distraction

- Never have distractions been so **voluminous, persistent, and additive**: email notifications, tabs, always-on devices, social streams, constant messaging.
- **It is an addiction.** Checking email/socials gives instant positive feedback with delayed negative feedback — the same reward loop as junk food or drugs.
- **It is an expectation.** Society expects constant availability; opting out looks like arrogance ("diva") or courage. We never chose this — we must redesign the system to suit us.
- **Creation is a separate process from consumption/communication.** Switching between them loses time and creative attention each time. *Solution: separate your day — a time for creating and a time for consuming/communicating.*

---

## 2. Focus Rituals (the daily operating system)

1. **Morning quiet** — start the day offline (coffee, meditation, run, or just sit) to rest the mind before the world intrudes.
2. **Start of day** — write the **3 Most Important Tasks (MITs)** (or the **One Thing**) on paper/a text file; *then immediately start the top one and single-task until done*. Do not check email first.
3. **Refocus ritual** — every hour or two, close browser/apps, take a 1–2 min breather, return to the MIT list and pick the next task before going online again.
4. **Alternate focus & rest** — like exercise intervals: 10/2, 25/5, 45/15 min; experiment for your own ratio.
5. **Alternate two focuses** — switching between two projects rests one cognitive muscle while the other works.
6. **Communicate first, then blocks** — 45 min of email/socials, then 2–4 h blocked (use a blocker) of pure focus; repeat.
7. **End of day** — review the day, note improvements, disconnect for the evening, choose tomorrow's focus.
8. **Weekly focus ritual** — 10 min weekly: review projects, simplify the to-do list, audit which rituals work.

> These are synonyms for the [[deep-work-attention-economics]] disciplines at a *ritual* granularity — the same protective cadence, scheduled daily.

---

## 3. Clearing Distractions

- **Limit the stream** — more connection → more distractions. Streams (email, IM, forums, news) must be rationed, batched, or eliminated.
- **You don't need to respond** — availability is not obligation; batch replies and let most of them wait.
- **Don't live in your inbox** — process at set times only; your inbox is not your to-do list.
- **The value of distraction** — some idle time is fine if it's *chosen*; the problem is when it interrupts creating.
- **Tools** — blocking software, disconnecting routers, working somewhere without wifi, leaving the phone behind, digital sabbaticals.
- Complementary: [[101-ways-workplace-productivity-fishel]] gives the low-tech workplace version (silent ringtones, no entertainment apps open, close unnecessary tabs).

---

## 4. Simplification (the supply side of focus)

- **A simpler life** = fewer possessions, fewer commitments, fewer distractions → *space* for what matters. Say "no": valuing your time above all else.
- **Do less, do it better** — narrow scope, offer less but better; busy-ness ≠ productivity.
- **Life's short list** — take 30–60 min and name the 4–5 things you love most (Babauta's: family, writing, reading, running); everything else is a candidate for elimination.
- **Mechanics** — pick one cluttered spot, sort into *use&love / donate / trash*, give everything a permanent home, expand 10 min/day. This is the personal-scale [[apo-handbook-productivity]] **5S** (Sort, Set in order, Shine, Standardize, Sustain).

---

## 5. The 3-Step System for Getting Amazing Things Done

1. **Find Something Amazing** to work on today — exciting, life-changing-in-a-small-way. You aren't committing for life, just for today; pick wrong and pick again tomorrow.
2. **Clear away everything else** — desk, computer (close programs), notifications off, phone off, meetings cleared. Make the one task the only possible object of focus.
3. **Focus on it as first thing** — nearest to waking as possible; resist the urge to check anything; repeat until you've banked 30 min–2 h (or finished it). Bask, then repeat with the next Something Amazing.

> This is [[productive-flowchart]] steps 5→7 in their purest form: one amazing MIT, deeply protected, executed immediately.

---

## 6. Single-Tasking

- Multitasking is *less efficient* (gear-switching), *more complicated* (stress/errors), and impossible — the brain handles one conscious stream.
- **Single-tasking life**: wake → move → MITs until done → then optional smaller tasks. Working on one thing until it's done, one at a time.
- Same conclusion as Newport's [[deep-work-attention-economics]] (attention residue) and Scott Young's "multitasking is the 8th deadly sin" ([[little-book-productivity-scott-young]]).
