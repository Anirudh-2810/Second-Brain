# Pricing & Packages

> Principle: price against the value of hours removed, not your time. A workflow saving 8 h/month at $25/h is worth $2,400/yr — charging $500–900 to build it is an easy client yes.

## Value Math (use in proposals)

```
Hours saved/month  ×  loaded hourly cost  ×  12  =  annual value
Your setup fee ≈ 20–40% of annual value
Retainer     ≈ 5–10% of annual value
```

Example: task takes 1 h/day, staff cost $20/h → $6,000/yr.
Setup: $900–1,500. Retainer: $99–149/mo.

## Three Core Packages (productized)

### STARTER — $297–$497
- 1 workflow, up to 4 nodes-chain steps
- Their accounts connected, tested
- Handoff video + 7 days bug cover

### GROWTH — $697–$1,497 *(most popular)*
- Up to 3 connected workflows or 1 complex pipeline
- Error-alerting + retry logic
- Documentation page, 2 revisions, 30 days support

### SCALE — $1,997+
- Multi-step system (e.g., lead → CRM → AI scoring → sales alerts)
- Self-hosted instance setup for them (they own infra)
- Training session for their team, 60 days support

## Getting Paid

- **Stripe payment links** — professional, cards, subscriptions (needs entity or sole-prop setup per country)
- **PayPal / Wise** — international clients, fast start
- Terms: 50/50 split standard; 100% upfront under $500; retainers billed 1st of month, auto-charge
- Always send a simple invoice PDF even with payment links (clients' accountants ask)

## Contract Clauses That Save You

1. **Scope**: list exact deliverables; "anything outside = separate quote"
2. **Revisions**: N rounds included; further changes billable
3. **Client dependencies**: delayed access/API keys pause the timeline
4. **IP**: workflow transfers on final payment; you retain right to reuse generic patterns
5. **No uptime guarantee** on third-party APIs; retainer covers response, not external outages
6. **Termination**: either side, 30 days notice on retainers

## Rate Raiser Schedule

- Every 3 delivered projects: raise package prices 10–15%
- Every 5 testimonials: add premium tier above SCALE
- Never discount — reduce scope instead ("I can do phase 1 within budget")
