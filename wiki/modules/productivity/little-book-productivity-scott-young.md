# The Little Book of Productivity (Scott Young)

> **Source:** [[raw-sources/_extracted/the-little-book-of-productivity-scott-young-6268.txt]] (Scott Young, *The Little Book of Productivity* — 7 parts, 99 tactics).

Seven fronts: beating procrastination, becoming organized, staying energized, getting things finished, automating routine, productivity hacks, and doing the right work. Everything is a *tactic*; the meta-move is turning tactics into habits.

---

## 1. Beating Procrastination

- **Weekly/Daily Goals** — end of week: list the work for the next 7 days; each night: pull today's chunk from the weekly list; **when daily goals are done, stop**.
- **Timeboxing** — 30–90 min timer, don't stop until the box ends or the task completes; the only early exit is finishing (yields the "sledgehammer of productivity tricks").
- **Dissolving Tasks** — vague projects breed procrastination; make the next 60 seconds obvious ("Write essay" → "Pick topic, find research material, create notes, write thesis").
- **Sprinting Theory** — willpower is a *drainable resource*; find each task's **discipline-critical range** and aim your willpower there (waking: hold 10 min; habits: 30 days; creative block: 20 more minutes).
- **Schedule calibration** — you must *trust* the list: when it says done, stop; adding tasks mid-way violates trust and breaks future motivation.
- **Building self-discipline muscles** — go 10 more minutes past the quitting threshold; extend sustained attention by 10–20%; resistance training for willpower.
- **Catalysts & roadblocks** — keep visible goals, make public commitments, use Post-Its; and check the *soil*: if chronic procrastination persists, goals/reasons may be dead (algorithm: still inspire? still meaningful? avoiding real fears?).
- Also: **mantras** ("Do it Now!"), **churning** (pump out garbage to break creative blocks), **distraction-free workplace**, **don't "should" yourself** (only *want* / *not going to do*), **Ready-Fire-Aim** (act, then correct — beats eternal planning), **quit procrastination vices** (eliminate or constrict).

> Note the dual 2-Minute invites: GTD's "do it now if <2 min" ([[gtd-task-management]]) and baby-step habit starts ([[atomic-habits-systems]]).

## 2. Becoming Organized

- **Organization is a skill** (design + maintenance), not a personality trait.
- **Less Mess = Less Stress** — organize your *physical environment* and your *tasks/work system* so the brain isn't the hard drive.
- **The Capture Device** — always at your side; "if you're wearing pants, you should have a capture device."
- **Give everything a home** — every physical item and every task/event/project has a place; mess = homeless piles.
- **The Simple Organizing System** — exactly **three piles**:
  - **Projects** — collections of tasks with a common goal (stable, updated least often).
  - **Tasks** — global to-do + weekly goals + daily goals (the active, checking pile).
  - **Events** — time-locked calendar items pushed onto the daily list on their day.
- **Branch Method** — regular reviews: split bloated piles, merge unused ones, spawn new ones as life changes (adaptive, not "perfect").
- **Write out your goals** — a binder you can check off; flipping through accomplished goals is fuel.
- Also: **communication logs**, **do what you say you will do** (record every promise), **tidy vs organized** (system, not looks), **reading notes**, **digital organization** (purge + separate archives).

> The three-pile system is a slimmed [[gtd-task-management]]; PARA ([[pkm-code-framework]]) is a four-pile variant (Projects/Areas/Resources/Archives).

## 3. Staying Energized

- **Energy is the limited resource, not time.** A 2-h task done exhausted can take 40 min clear-headed.
- **Morning ritual** (30–60 min, waketime consistent) — include exercise; avoid passive brain use; wake up *when you decided the night before*.
- **Take a day off** — work 6 not 7 days; recovery compounds and cuts procrastination (burnout ↔ procrastination).
- **Exercise routine** — the highest-ROI investment; aerobic + anaerobic; morning run upgrades your battery (AA → D).
- **Eating for energy** — low-glycemic foods (steady release), 4–6 small meals, carry a water bottle; avoid caffeine-as-water.
- **Make your time top-heavy** — hardest, longest, least-loved tasks first, after the ritual; weekly top-heaviness: more Monday, Sunday off.
- **Work in cycles** — sprint/recover; assign time-off *first*; schedule calibration enforces it.
- **Close open loops** — every task needs a defined stopping point or procrastination/burnout follows.
- Also: **15-Minute Rule** (commit +15 min through slumps), **recharge motivation** (review goals), **stay sharp** (mental fitness: info diet + always be learning), **work/play schedules** (you're a workplace Olympian, not a couch potato), **balance creative muscles** (switch task types when drained).

## 4. Getting Things Finished + Automating Routine

- **Complete projects, not tasks** — productivity is *accomplishment*, not activity; project focus over task-filing.
- **Set (soft) deadlines** — deadlines are a budget for time; self-set soft deadlines *ahead* of hard ones break exam-crunch pile-ups.
- **Parkinson's Law / Hofstadter's Rule** — "work expands to the time given," but complex work always takes longer than expected; *set firm value-based deadlines, expect fewer features than your ideal*.
- **Reward laziness** — Productivity ≠ work; eliminate low-value tasks, simplify, work less → do more.
- **Skeleton planning** — plan only the rigid decisions; leave flexible ones open (avoids $15-vs-3-months mistakes).
- **Rapid prototyping / Don't reinvent the wheel / Outsourcing** — test cheap, reuse, buy leverage.
- **Forced productivity / Daily checkmarks / Exit strategies** — pressure + a visible checklist; define what happens *after* done or you'll never ship.
- **The 30-Day Trial** (30DT) — one habit, 30 days; slip = restart Day 1; after day 30 it's conditioned. Applied to: diet, exercise, GTD, batching email.
- **Triggers** — signal → 30–60 s ritual that launches the rest of the habit.
- **Replacement theory** — new habits must fill the emotional need of the old one or they'll fail.
- **One habit at a time · Be consistent · Ratchet theory · Unbreakable standard · Experiment** — focus, daily repetition, small increments, no-quit-first-30-days, and accept that 30–50% of trials become permanent.
- **Routine vs non-routine** — automate the *process* of routine tasks; spend mental energy on non-routine ones.

## 5. Productivity Hacks & Doing the Right Work

- **The Daily Six** — write tasks 1–6, start at #1; even a whole day on #1 is optimal.
- **Know what you want** — the 5 questions before any project (why / done shape / required features / primary / secondary goals).
- **Separate planning from doing** — plan in reviews; act outside them.
- **Measure · Timelog · Pareto's 80/20 · To-Stop lists · Fail quickly · Ruthless feedback** — measure what matters, zap illusions, filter to the vital 20%, stop the non-vital, seek feedback early (constant beta).
- **Be a hedgehog / "T" model** — one deep skill + broad shallow bar.
- **Hard work isn't important** — the skill is balancing *working hard* with *avoiding hard work* (leverage, skills, re-framing).
- **Productivity with a purpose** — work on a passion, something meaningful, on improving.
